# RESHAPE — النوم مو رفاهية… النوم صحة
## Scientific references & claim audit

Prepared for the RESHAPE sleep-health awareness film (48.5 s, 9:16).
All four primary studies were cross-checked against their PubMed records / journal
abstracts via web search on 2026-10-27 (direct PubMed fetches are blocked from this
production environment, so verification used the indexed abstract text and the
publishers' secondary records). Nothing in the film goes beyond what the abstracts state.

---

### Study A — Chronic sleep restriction and cognitive performance
**Van Dongen HPA, Maislin G, Mullington JM, Dinges DF.** *The cumulative cost of additional
wakefulness: dose-response effects on neurobehavioral functions and sleep physiology from
chronic sleep restriction and total sleep deprivation.* **Sleep.** 2003;26(2):117–126.
DOI: [10.1093/sleep/26.2.117](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/26.2.117) · PubMed: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12683469/>

- **Design:** randomised laboratory experiment; 48 healthy adults (21–38 y).
  Sleep restriction arm: 4 h, 6 h or 8 h time-in-bed per night for 14 consecutive days;
  separate total-sleep-deprivation arm (3 nights).
- **Findings used:** restriction to 6 h or less produced cumulative, dose-dependent
  deficits in neurobehavioural performance (attention lapses on the Psychomotor Vigilance
  Task, working memory). Subjective sleepiness ratings rose only modestly — participants
  were largely unaware of how much performance had declined.
- **Limitations:** small, young, healthy sample; laboratory setting; outcomes are
  neurobehavioural tests, not real-world accidents or productivity.
- **In the film:** *"دراسة لقت إن ست ساعات نوم لأسبوعين ممكن تضعف تركيزك، حتى لو ما انتبهت"*
  — experimental, so "can weaken your focus" is causal language justified by the design;
  "ممكن" (may) keeps it from over-generalising to every person. Label: `VAN DONGEN ET AL. — SLEEP, 2003`.
  Visual: metaphorical neural network slowing down + a 14-day bar with a downward
  attention line. No brain-scan imagery and no percentages are shown.

### Study B — Sleep duration and susceptibility to the common cold
**Prather AA, Janicki-Deverts D, Hall MH, Cohen S.** *Behaviorally assessed sleep and
susceptibility to the common cold.* **Sleep.** 2015;38(9):1353–1359.
DOI: [10.5665/sleep.4968](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.4968) · PubMed: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26118561/>

- **Design:** 164 healthy adults (18–55 y); 7 nights of wrist actigraphy + diaries, then
  quarantine and controlled nasal rhinovirus exposure, monitored 5 days for a clinical cold.
- **Findings used:** shorter measured sleep was associated with higher odds of developing
  a clinical cold (OR 4.50, 95% CI 1.08–18.69 for <5 h; OR 4.24, 95% CI 1.08–16.71 for
  5–6 h; vs >7 h). Associations held after adjusting for antibody titres, demographics,
  season, BMI, psychological variables and health practices.
- **Limitations:** observational for the sleep exposure (exposure to the virus was
  controlled, sleep was not randomised); wide confidence intervals; single pathogen.
- **In the film:** *"ودراسة ثانية ربطت قلة النوم بزيادة احتمال الإصابة بالزكام"* — "linked /
  associated with a higher likelihood". No odds ratio is quoted on screen (an OR is not an
  absolute probability), no claim that sleep prevents illness. Label: `PRATHER ET AL. — SLEEP, 2015`.
  Visual: abstract shield + particles; the on-screen hours bar drops from 8 h to 5 h to
  mirror the exposure categories; "١٦٤ شخص" is not shown to keep the scene uncluttered.

### Study C — Sleep duration and cardiovascular outcomes
**Cappuccio FP, Cooper D, D'Elia L, Strazzullo P, Miller MA.** *Sleep duration predicts
cardiovascular outcomes: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies.*
**European Heart Journal.** 2011;32(12):1484–1492.
DOI: [10.1093/eurheartj/ehr007](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehr007) · PubMed: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21300732/>

- **Design:** systematic review + meta-analysis of 15 prospective studies (24 cohort
  samples), 474,684 participants, follow-up 6.9–25 years, 16,067 events.
- **Findings used:** short sleep was associated with higher risk of coronary heart
  disease (RR 1.48, 95% CI 1.22–1.80) and stroke (RR 1.15, 1.00–1.31). Long sleep was also
  associated with CHD (RR 1.38), stroke (RR 1.65) and total CVD (RR 1.41).
- **Limitations:** observational cohorts; self-reported sleep duration in most studies;
  residual confounding; the authors describe sleep duration as a *predictor or marker*
  of cardiovascular outcomes, not an established cause.
- **In the film:** *"والأبحاث تربط النوم غير الكافي بمخاطر تخص صحة القلب"* — "research
  links". On-screen chip: *"ارتباط في دراسات رصدية — مو إثبات سبب مباشر"* (association in
  observational studies, not proof of direct causation) and *"قلة النوم ارتبطت بخطر أعلى
  لأمراض القلب التاجية"*. Label: `CAPPUCCIO ET AL. — EUR HEART J, 2011`.
  Visual: stylised heart icon (not anatomical claim), ECG motif, dashed "association" lines
  from sleep-night bars to the heart.

### Study D — Sleep regularity and mortality
**Windred DP, Burns AC, Lane JM, Saxena R, Rutter MK, Cain SW, Phillips AJK.** *Sleep
regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: a prospective
cohort study.* **Sleep.** 2024;47(1):zsad253 (published online 21 Sep 2023).
DOI: [10.1093/sleep/zsad253](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253) · PubMed: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37738616/>

- **Design:** prospective cohort; 60,977 UK Biobank participants (mean age ≈62.8 y, 55%
  female); Sleep Regularity Index computed from ~10 million hours of accelerometer data;
  mortality follow-up (~7.8 years median).
- **Findings used:** more regular sleep was associated with 20–48% lower all-cause
  mortality (top four SRI quintiles vs the least regular), after adjustment for age, sex,
  ethnicity, sociodemographic, lifestyle and health factors; regularity predicted mortality
  more strongly than duration.
- **Limitations:** observational; UK Biobank is older and less diverse than the general
  population; regularity has not been experimentally shown to extend life.
- **In the film:** *"وانتظام نومك بعد مهم"* — "the regularity of your sleep also matters".
  No mortality figures are spoken or shown; chip: *"دراسة متابعة على ~٦١ ألف شخص"*.
  Label: `WINDRED ET AL. — SLEEP, 2024`. Visual: weekly calendar snapping into a steady rhythm.

### Clinical recommendations used for the practical-advice scene
- **Watson NF et al. (AASM & Sleep Research Society consensus, 2015).** *Recommended amount
  of sleep for a healthy adult.* Sleep 2015;38(6):843–844 / J Clin Sleep Med 2015;11(6):591–592.
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979105/> — adults 18–60 y should sleep **7 or more
  hours per night on a regular basis**; individual needs vary and >9 h may be appropriate
  for some (young adults, recovery from sleep debt, illness).
- **American Heart Association — Life's Essential 8 / "Enjoy a good night's sleep".**
  <https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/healthy-lifestyle/sleep/enjoy-a-good-nights-sleep>
  — adults should aim for **7–9 hours**; consistent timing, quality and routine matter, not
  hours alone.
- **CDC — About Sleep.** <https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/> — adults need **at least 7 hours**;
  keep a consistent schedule, make the bedroom quiet/dark/comfortable, avoid caffeine late
  in the day, limit screens before bed.

**Film wording:** *"إذا أنت بالغ، حاول تنام سبع إلى تسع ساعات، وثبّت وقت نومك وصحوتك، وخفّف
الكافيين بالمساء"* — the 7–9 h range (AHA) sits inside the AASM "7 or more" guidance; the
card sub-line *"الاحتياج يختلف من شخص لآخر"* states that needs vary, so the film does not
imply every adult needs the same amount.
